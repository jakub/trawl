# An investigation is grant-visible and its evidence outlives the corpus

status: accepted (2026-10-22), prep record for the MCP 1.0 slices (C1 and C2 of the arc #214 → A1 → A2 → B, C1 → C2); human rulings of 2026-10-22 recorded below

An agent investigating through the MCP server (ADR-0058) holds its own key. The human watching from a phone holds another. Saved queries are key-owned (`saved_queries.key_id`, ADR-0004), so a key-owned investigation would be invisible to the person it is for. ADR-0035 already decided that installation objects are visible by grant, never by ownership, for the 1.1 tables, nets and analyses. The investigation adopts that pattern now, for one object, and is the first installation-owned object in trawl. It is also the thing that makes the agent's work inspectable: the server records what ran, the agent records why, and the human sees both without a chat transcript.

## Decision

**The investigation is an installation-owned object.** Postgres holds it in an appended trawl migration: `investigations` (a random 128-bit id, title, question, scope text, status open or closed, a sequence number, `retain_until`, and `created_by` and `updated_by` as key id plus a name snapshot that outlives key revocation), an append-only `investigation_log` whose entries carry a per-investigation `seq` and a kind (`step`, `keep`, `finding`, `finding_status`, `note`, `status`), the actor, and a bounded payload, and `captured_results.investigation_id` to tie ADR-0057's results to it. It is organisational: it is not in the table, net and analysis namespace, cannot be read by `from`, and never schedules anything. A step is not a run; a run is an execution of a net.

**Visibility and authority come from two new permissions (amends ADR-0006).** `investigation_read` with `query` lists and reads every investigation in the installation, its steps, findings and kept evidence; the human ruled that out of the two readable models (installation-wide, or opener-plus-operators) the first is right, because `query` is already flat over every environment and fleet-auth has no per-object grants, so anything narrower would be fiction. `investigation_write` with the two above creates, attaches work, keeps rows, writes findings and notes, and closes or reopens any investigation; the human ruled that any writer may close or reopen, and that creator identity is attribution, never a gate. Cancelling another key's running work stays with `server_manage`, as today: board access grants no cancellation power. Deleting an investigation and extending evidence past the ceiling below need `server_manage`. The permissions are enum variants mirrored in `PERMISSION_NAMES`, registered as vocabulary by an appended fleet-auth migration, and granted to roles only by an explicit `fleet-admin roles add-perm`; no role is seeded or silently re-permissioned. The agent's key is minted from a documented recipe in `operate/access.md`: one service-kind key on one role holding `query`, `schema_read`, `validate`, `investigation_read` and `investigation_write`, with `rate_rpm` 60 and a 90-day expiry, and without `stream`, `export`, `saved_query` or `query_cancel`; the human confirmed each value. A settings-page minting control is not part of 1.0.

**The server records steps; the client records reasons.** A capture (ADR-0057) or correlation read (ADR-0058) that names an investigation needs `investigation_write`, an open investigation and an evidence reservation, all checked before any executor permit is taken; the step is appended at admission with the executed text, the bounds, the request id and the ADR-0046 facts, and its terminal outcome is written when the work ends, failures and client aborts included, by a completion that does not die with the request. The agent supplies short purposes and findings. A finding has a statement, a status of hypothesis, supported, refuted or unresolved, references to captured rows, optional counterevidence and an optional remaining question; its status is the author's assertion, visibly attributed, and trawl validates that the references exist and are readable, not that they prove the sentence. No chat transcript and no hidden reasoning are stored.

**Attaching retains the whole result; a keep circles rows in it.** The human ruled that attaching a search to an investigation promotes its whole captured result to kept evidence, with no copy, and that a keep records row numbers and a reason against it; every reader sees the whole result, which they could have queried themselves. A keep of a row in the caller's own temporary result promotes that result the same way. Kept evidence lives in the results root of ADR-0057 under its file modes and publication rule, and the evidence budget counts each distinct result's file bytes once. Kept evidence is a second copy of event data and outlives corpus retention, so the board still shows the rows after the source logs expire.

**Two clocks (amends ADR-0018).** Evidence files live 7 days after the investigation's last change and never past 30 days after its creation, unless `server_manage` extends them; budgets are 64 MiB per investigation and 1 GiB per installation, reserved before scanning and refused before any permit when they cannot be. The sweep deletes files and leaves metadata: a kept row reads as pruned and the finding still cites it. The investigation's own text, its steps, findings, notes and keeps, stays until an operator deletes the investigation; the human ruled that the case notes outlive the photocopies. All numbers are configuration and are advertised by the capabilities route.

**Edits are sequenced, and only overwrites can conflict.** Every write advances one sequence per investigation. Appends (step, keep, finding, note) carry no expected sequence, because they overwrite nothing and an agent's keep must not fail because the human wrote a note. Overwrites (status, finding status, title, question) carry the sequence they observed and fail with 409 `sequence_conflict` and `{expected, current}` when it moved; the client re-reads and retries. Create is create-if-absent on an id the MCP server mints, never the model, so a lost response is recovered by sending the same request again; the same creator with the same payload gets the existing investigation, anything else 409. These are the shapes ADR-0035 names for table writes, adopted early.

**The live board is pull, both ways.** The human's page polls `read since=<seq>` every 5 seconds while the tab is visible and backs off while hidden, shows the question, the latest finding, live activity, then findings with their evidence and the steps, and inserts new entries without moving the scroll position. A tap on Keep records a keep under the human's key with a reason; Send to investigation in Search runs a capture attached to the investigation under the human's key, so the server, never the browser, attests evidence. The agent learns about the human the same way: the MCP server holds a `seen_seq` per investigation per session, sends it with every attached call, and every attached tool result carries the entries written since it by other actors, bounded, with a continuation. No SSE route and no `subscriptions/listen` ship in 1.0: no client documents consuming listen, SSE slots are shared with live tail and uncounted after the head, and the sequence read heals missed updates anyway.

**Controls are absent without the grant (ADR-0025, ADR-0031).** The investigations list and page, Keep, Send to investigation, close and reopen appear only for a key holding the permission that authorises them.

Considered and rejected:

- **Key-owned investigations like saved queries.** The reader and the writer are different principals by construction.
- **Per-object grants.** fleet-auth has roles and permissions, not object ACLs; inventing them for one object would prejudge the 1.1 model and delay the slice.
- **Copy only the kept rows.** A row action would then publish nothing else, but it copies, races expiry, and leaves the human without the neighbouring rows when keeping from the board. Readers hold `query` anyway.
- **Expected sequence on every edit.** Two writers on one board would conflict on appends that lose nothing.
- **The model mints the create id.** Models produce non-random ids that collide across sessions.
- **Purge the investigation text 30 days after its evidence.** The human wrote those findings; bounded storage comes from the evidence clock.
- **A server-side cursor per key.** Two sessions on one key would consume each other's updates, and a read must stay a read.

## Amendment: decided while building #310, 2026-10-25

Building #310 settled points the decision left open and refined some it stated. The decision stands; these refine it.

- **Title and question are entry kinds.** The log's kinds are `step`, `keep`, `finding`, `finding_status`, `note`, `status`, `title` and `question`. An overwrite of the title or the question writes an entry like any other change, so the board shows who renamed the case and when.
- **An actor is a key prefix and a name, never a key id.** The wire attributes every investigation and entry by `{prefix, name}`, both snapshots taken at the write, which still read after the key is renamed or revoked. Fleet's surrogate key id stays server side, for the create replay check and for leaving the caller's own entries out of `activity`. `/whoami` and the captured-result wire already attribute by prefix, and an investigation should not be the exception.
- **A PATCH changes one field.** `PATCH /api/v1/investigations/{id}` carries exactly one of `title`, `question` and `status` under its `expected_seq`, and anything else is 400. Two overwrites under one expected sequence would need an order between them, such as a close and a title change in one request, and no 1.0 client sends more than one.
- **Authority before shape, everywhere.** Every authenticated handler checks its permission before it reads its path, query string or body, so a key without the permission gets 403 whatever it sent and only a permitted key learns that its request did not parse. This began with the investigation and evidence routes and became a server-wide rule, guarded by a source test that refuses a bare `Path`, `Query` or `Json` parameter on an authenticated handler. The body limit follows the same rule: it is judged where the handler reads the body, behind authentication and the authority check, and keeps its 413 envelope and its two settings.

  *Amended 2026-10-25 (#310 security review): a handler that reads a body names its permission with `policy::Authorized<needs::…>`, a request-parts extractor that axum runs before the body extractor, as `/ingest`'s `IngestKey` already did. So trawld never reads the body of a request whose key lacks the route's permission, and a client that never finishes such a body holds no request place. The source guard requires an authority extractor ahead of every body parameter.*

  *Amended 2026-10-25 (#310 final sweep): the attached capture is the one place a grant follows the body. `POST /api/v1/results` names `query` ahead of its body, but whether a capture attaches is the body's `investigation` field, so trawld checks `investigation_write` once it has read the body: the intent to attach is in the body. Only reading the body, under its limit, and its JSON shape come before that 403. No query text, purpose, bound, step or admission work does, so a key that holds `query` without `investigation_write` learns only whether its body reads as a capture request. The human kept the one route and its body field rather than add a route for attached captures. The same sweep bounded the outcomes that wait for the step writer. An attached capture takes a place in its key's share, `[capture] per_key_running + queue_depth` places, the size of its lane, before its step is appended, and answers 429 `capture_busy` with `Retry-After: 1` and no step at once when every place is held. It does not wait for a place: a waiting capture is a live request that holds a request slot, so one key's captures waiting behind a stalled outcome write could fill `[server] max_concurrent_requests` and refuse every other key's requests. A per-key cap on waiters was rejected, since the waiters would still hold slots. Outside a stall, the share is full only when the key's lane is, or an outcome is a moment from being written, which the one-second retry covers. The place goes back only once the outcome is written, so a `COMMIT` whose answer is withheld holds at most a share of each key's outcomes behind it, and a key whose outcome writes stall holds no more than its share and leaves every other key its own. The share is the writer's only bound: the installation's bound is that share for each key that writes, and the operator mints the keys. No installation-wide count of places sits above the shares, because a count sized for one key binds several keys' normal work while their lanes would admit it, and no count follows from a number of keys trawld does not know. The same sweep took evidence past its retention out of the budgets without waiting for the sweep: before the binding evidence pass counts, it prunes the kept results of every investigation whose `retain_until` has passed at the gate's clock, then counts every kept row, so the sweep's cadence never decides whether a keep fits. Leaving those rows out of the count without pruning them was rejected. A write takes its `now` before its reservation reads the clock, so a write dated before the deadline slides it and revives the rows beside the charge admitted in their place, past either budget. A pruned row never becomes kept again. The evidence pre-check, which only refuses, still leaves those rows out without pruning them.*
- **Attached results are kept from birth, and promotion happens under the investigation's lock.** An attached capture commits its result `kept`, its step's outcome and the investigation's next sequence in one transaction, and charges the evidence budgets, never the temporary quota. A keep or a finding that cites the caller's own live temporary result promotes it in place inside the append's transaction, which locks the investigation row first. A citation of a result another investigation keeps is 409; every other result that cannot be cited answers one identical 400, so a refusal tells the caller nothing a read would not. Promotion outside that lock could commit evidence into an investigation whose prune had already run.
- **A promotion's charge outlives its request.** The evidence reservation and the transaction that promotes run in a task the request awaits but does not own, as a capture's commit does, so a client that disconnects after the commit was sent cannot release a charge whose row then lands above the budget.
- **A write that outlives its request keeps the request's count and waits no later than its deadline.** An attached capture's step admission and a promotion's transaction run in tasks the request does not own. Each task keeps the request's place in `max_concurrent_requests` until its write ends, including the `aborted` outcome of a step whose request went away. This is a second exception beside the body work of ADR-0054, for the same reason: a client must not hang up and start the same write again past the limit. Each waits for a connection and for the locks no later than the capture's deadline, or the query timeout for a promotion, and then answers 429 `capture_busy` with nothing written. The transaction sets its own `lock_timeout` to the same instant, so Postgres stops waiting too. The commit is never cut short.

  *Amended 2026-10-25 (#310 round-three review): Postgres gives each lock wait the whole `lock_timeout` anew, so the transaction arms it from what remains of the deadline before every statement that can wait, and the waits together end at the deadline. A transaction that does not commit rolls back and waits for Postgres to answer before the task gives the count back, because a dropped transaction only queues its `ROLLBACK`.*

  *Amended 2026-10-25 (#310 round-three review): the `aborted` outcome of a step whose request went away keeps the count no later than the capture's deadline, not until its write ends. Until the deadline it waits for a connection and the lock inside the count. Past the deadline the count goes back and the write finishes outside it, so the step is still written and never stays running until a restart.*

  *Amended 2026-10-25 (#310 round-four review): `lock_timeout` bounds each lock wait, not the statement, so an update that waits on one row after another, or a statement that waits after an earlier one did, could still wait past the deadline. Every statement of a bounded write runs under a `statement_timeout` set from what remains of the deadline before it, which covers all of the statement's waits, and the transaction no longer sets `lock_timeout`. The timeout is lifted before the commit, so the commit is still never cut short.*

  *Amended 2026-10-25 (#310 round-four review): the task keeps the count on a lease. The task gives it back when its write ends, and the deadline takes it back whatever the task awaits then, such as the unlink of pruned evidence or an awaited `BEGIN` or `COMMIT`. So the deadline is the latest the count goes back, even when the rollback's answer comes later; Postgres stops waiting at the same instant, under its own statement timeout.*

  *Amended 2026-10-25 (#310 round-four review): the step outcomes no request waits for, those a last handle's drop leaves and those handed over past the deadline, are written by one writer task in the order they arrive. Each attempt is bounded by `[capture] deadline_secs`, and one that runs out goes behind the others and is tried again until it is written. However many steps are abandoned against a held lock, their writes hold at most one connection and one lock wait, where each used to take a connection of its own until the pool ran dry. Of the outcomes written apart from a kept result, only boot's interruptions still wait unbounded.*

  *Amended 2026-10-25 (#310 round-five review): under a deadline, every await of a write before its commit is bounded on both sides: by Postgres, under the `statement_timeout`, and by the client, which stops awaiting the connection, the `BEGIN` and each statement at the deadline, so an answer that never comes ends the write there too. A write the client cuts short closes its session instead of returning it to the pool, since a rollback would first have to read the answer it stopped awaiting, and Postgres ends the transaction with the session. A deadline already passed refuses the next statement and the commit before anything is sent. The commit alone is never cut short. So the deadline is the latest the request's count is kept, not the instant all work stops: a commit sent before it lands, and a rollback, the unlink of pruned evidence and an outcome handed to the writer may finish after it, outside the count. No connection returns to either production pool inside a transaction: the pool closes one that does.*

  *Amended 2026-10-25 (#310 round-six review): under a deadline, every await of a write ends at the deadline, the rollback included, and only a `COMMIT` already sent is awaited past it, since cutting a commit short would leave the write's outcome unknown. A rollback that the deadline leaves no time to send, or whose answer has not come by the deadline, closes the session instead, as a cut does. A session goes back to the pool only after Postgres has answered its rollback or commit. The deadline is checked again once the lift of the timeout is answered, because an answer already waiting is taken however late the task next runs, so no `COMMIT` is sent past the deadline. A rollback therefore no longer finishes after the deadline. An attempt of the step writer ends at its bound too, cut short or refused, and goes behind the others, with the one exception above: a `COMMIT` it has already sent is awaited until Postgres answers, and the outcomes queued behind it wait with it for as long as that one answer is withheld.*
- **A write past the deadline prunes first, then slides.** Every write locks the investigation and, when its `retain_until` has passed, prunes its kept evidence exactly as the sweep would before it writes and slides the clock. So a read that answered pruned stays true after any later write, and the sweep's hourly cadence never decides what survives.
- **A kept result past its retention reads 410 with its own reason.** Whether the sweep, boot, a write or a read reached it first, a kept result past its investigation's `retain_until` answers `result_gone` with "the investigation's evidence retention ended", and it stays 410 for as long as its investigation exists, because a finding still cites it.
- **Boot writes what it interrupted and slides nothing.** A step still running when trawld stopped is marked `interrupted` at the next start, before any capture is admitted. That write advances the sequence but not the evidence clock, since no principal changed anything.
- **The capture answer names its step.** An attached capture's answer carries `step_seq`, the sequence of the step it appended, so the MCP server can start following other actors' entries from the first attached search instead of the next write.
- **The MCP tool acknowledges every accepted write.** A reply to a write trawld accepted always names the id and the entry's sequence, at any response budget; the echoed entry and the board header are abbreviated to fit, with a marker that says where to read them whole. A write that committed must never read as a failure that invites a second write.
- **Overwrites carry no `seen_seq` and return no activity.** `PATCH` and the finding-status `PUT` take no cursor, so a `status` or `finding_status` call prints no activity. Nothing is lost: the session's `seen` stays where it was, and the next append or attached search prints what those calls skipped, exactly once. Adding the cursor to overwrites is follow-up work.
- **Only captures attach in this slice.** `POST /api/v1/results` takes `investigation`; the correlation reads of #309 do not yet. Whichever of #309 and #310 lands second wires attachment for the other.

  *Amended 2026-10-26 (#309): context, sightings and compare take `investigation` as `POST /api/v1/results` does and run through the one capture core, so authority (checked before the read's own validation), the purpose bound, the step, the evidence admission, `commit_kept`, `step_seq` and `activity` are the search's. A refusal of the read's own request, such as a cap, a focus lookup, a field, a window or a filter, comes after authority and the purpose bound and before the step, so it records no step. A kept result's facts document counts in the evidence budgets with its file, at admission's commit and on promotion by a keep or a finding, and leaves with its file when the result expires or is pruned.*

## Amendment: decided while building #311, 2026-10-26

Building the live board settled points the decision left open and corrected one it stated. The decision stands; these refine it.

- **A hidden tab reads nothing.** The decision said the board backs off while hidden; #311's acceptance criterion says polling stops, and the criterion wins. While `document.hidden` the board sends no read. When the tab shows again it reads at once, then every 5 seconds. Backoff is for failures only and is separate: a failed read waits 5, 10, 20, 40, then at most 60 seconds, and a 429 waits at least its `Retry-After`. One board read is in flight at a time, and a poll never marks the board busy. A 403 on a read stops the board's reads for the visit and removes its write controls, a 404 ends the visit as missing, and a 401 goes to sign-in. None of them is retried.
- **"Does not move the scroll position" means the content stays put, not `scrollTop`.** A change the reader did not cause, a poll or a cited row landing, leaves the element that holds the reader's place at the same viewport offset, so the page changes `scrollTop` by however far content grew above it. The page owns the rule, with `overflow-anchor: none` on its scroller: it picks the first keyed element still showing below the scroller's top before the change applies and restores that element's offset after. A person's own scroll or disclosure is never corrected. Entries, findings and steps are keyed by the `seq` that created them, so a step outcome or a finding-status change updates in place and never closes an open disclosure. Keeping `scrollTop` fixed was rejected: content inserted above the reader would push what they read down the screen, which is the movement the criterion forbids.
- **Send on a moving range pins, re-runs, then sends (human ruling 2026-10-26).** A capture is immutable and its window is absolute, so a Search with a quick range or an open end cannot be sent as it is. The Send dialog says the range moves and offers to pin it to the accepted response's `execution.started_at`, the server's clock and never the phone's: a quick range of length `d` becomes the whole-second hull of `[started_at − d, started_at]`, and an open end ends at `started_at`. Pinning navigates Search through the shared codec to the absolute range and runs it again; once that execution is accepted, the dialog sends that pinned search, so the person sees the rows that are sent. A fixed absolute range sends at once. No server change. Rejected: pinning silently at `started_at` and sending, because the capture would then be asked for rows nobody looked at; and a server-side bounds field in the query response, because the pin needs no new wire.
- **Any 409 on an overwrite is the conflict.** The store checks that the investigation is open before it compares the sequence, so a stale close can answer `investigation_closed` as well as `sequence_conflict`. The board treats both the same: a persistent notice that nothing changed, an immediate read through the same controller with the reader's place kept, and the person acts again on the control that now stands. It never retries by itself.
- **A write whose outcome is unknown is never sent again by the page.** Appends are not idempotent. A keep, close, reopen or send whose answer was lost (a network failure, a 502 or 504, or a 5xx without trawld's envelope) reads as outcome unknown, keeps what the person entered, offers a refresh, and holds its control until a read begun after the write lands. A success stays acknowledged even if the read after it fails. The board's cursor moves only with a read it accepted, never with a write's response.
- **Keep lives in the evidence viewer, one row per tap.** A keep needs the row in front of the person, so the Keep column is in the viewer over the captured result, not on the board's cited rows. Each keep records one row with a required reason, checked before sending against the server's bound (trimmed, non-empty, at most 1024 bytes of UTF-8, no NUL). It carries no `seen_seq` and no expected sequence. The column is absent without `investigation_write`, while the investigation is closed, and on a result that does not read. Multi-row keep from the browser is not in 1.0.
- **Send to investigation is absent in live mode and for `from saved` (guessed fork).** The control is present only in snapshot mode, on an accepted response with execution facts, for a session with `investigation_write`. A live stream has no window to capture, and a `from saved` search reads report rows with no time bounds, so neither yields a capture the server can attest; the control is absent rather than present and refusing (ADR-0025, ADR-0031). Rejected: sending a `from saved` search's stored sources with no window, which would capture something other than the rows on screen.
